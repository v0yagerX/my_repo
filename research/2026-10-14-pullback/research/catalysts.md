7. **The market's July reaction function: capex plus revenue acceleration is rewarded; capex without it is punished.**

- 2026 revenue growth raised to "slightly above 40%" in USD, from >30%. **Options and price**
- IBKR Oct 16 430 straddle about $16.0, ±3.7% from the Oct 14 close of $430.00.
- TSM ADR traded $424.30 overnight (−1.3%, IBKR 11:01 pm ET).
- TSM is −11.5% from its 52-week closing high of $485.80.

**What it signals for the pullback**
- **Good:**
  - Q4 guide implying another sequential rise in USD.
  - Gross margin guided at or above about 65% despite the 2nm ramp and overseas fabs.
  - 2026 growth lifted to the mid-40s.
  - Capex at or above the top of $60–64bn, with early 2027 commentary of "growth faster than prior three years."
  - Explicit AI-accelerator and CoWoS (advanced packaging) "demand > supply" language.
  - Read-through to NVDA: TSMC makes almost all of NVDA's chips, so a strong guide confirms Rubin wafer and packaging volume. Note that in July a strong print still drew a −5% TSM move and a two-day −6% SOXX move, because capex spooked investors.
- **Bad:**
  - Q4 guide flat or down QoQ.
  - Gross margin guide below about 63–64%.
  - Any "inventory digestion" or customer order-adjustment language.
  - A capex cut.
  - Hedging on 2027 AI demand.
  - Any of these would strongly support the deeper-correction or trend-break scenarios in semis.
- **Tape test (more important than the numbers):**
  - TSM and SOXX close green on Oct 15 and hold through Oct 16 expiration: first sign of seller exhaustion.
  - Gap-up that fades (as with ORCL on Sep 11 and NVDA on Oct 9): confirms distribution.

**Signal**

  - A shift of spend toward custom silicon: Microsoft unveiled Maia 300 on Oct 12, "potentially" 1M+ units in 2027 (oct8_narrative.md). OpenAI said its in-house "Jalapeno" chip beat NVDA's current lineup excluding Rubin (Euronews, Aug 26).
  - Free-cash-flow deterioration that forces financing.
  - **Ambiguous-to-bearish:** capex up but the spender's stock down (the GOOGL/META July pattern). Orders hold, but the market de-rates the whole AI-capex chain, which compresses NVDA's multiple even with estimates rising.

## 6. Implications for the pullback-bottom question

**Where the next 5 weeks' catalysts point.** The catalysts are dense, front-loaded and binary:
1. TSMC (Oct 15).
2. The Oct 21 and Oct 28–29 hyperscaler and semi-cap cluster, with FOMC on Oct 28.
3. AMD and Arm (Nov 3–4).
4. CoreWeave (about Nov 10).
5. NVDA (Nov 17/18).

The fundamental data in hand are uniformly strong: TSMC, ASML, Micron, NVDA's guide, and capex guidance. **The problem is price, not data.** The market has sold three consecutive beat-and-raise prints (ASML, MU, TSMC in July) and two good-news gap-ups (ORCL Sep 11, NVDA Oct 9).

**Bearish reading**
- Selling good news while the data remain strong is what late-cycle distribution looks like.
- The narrative has moved from "is demand there?" to two other questions:
  - Is the return on capex there? This is the GOOGL/META July punishment, the Hock Tan "casino" comment, and the Oct 8 OpenAI revenue reset.
  - Who funds it? This covers CoreWeave's bonds, NVDA's $105bn guarantee, $279bn of supply commitments, and "roughly a quarter" of next year's business from balance-sheet-backed AI labs.
- No earnings beat answers those questions, so catalysts can keep failing even as estimates rise. That is the mechanism for the **deeper-correction or trend-break scenario in semis**.
- NVDA's own post-earnings base rate (7 of 9 lower five sessions later) argues against the November print being *the* low.
- Under this reading, the low comes either before the print (an October washout around the hyperscaler cluster) or after it (a post-print drift into late November/December).

**Bullish reading**
- Every hard number says 2027 AI spend is going up: ASML sold out for 2027–28, TSMC +51% YoY, Micron +13% QoQ guide, NVDA +70% FY28 supply-constrained, Kress at $1.3tn 2027 capex against a Street of about $1tn.
- NVDA is only about 9% below its high, with low implied volatility (52-week IV percentile 15%). It is behaving as the *quality* hide-out within a broken SOXX (−20.7% from its June high), not as the epicentre.
- If hyperscalers on Oct 21–29 raise 2027 capex *and their own stocks rise* (the MSFT/AMZN July pattern), the "return on capex" fear is answered directly. NVDA would likely make its low before or into that cluster, a shallow-pullback outcome, and run into its print.

**Evidence that would distinguish the two in real time**
1. **TSMC tape on Oct 15–16.** A beat-and-raise that closes green and holds favours exhaustion. A fade favours distribution.
2. **Hyperscaler reaction function on Oct 21, 28 and 29.** Capex up with the spender's stock up is bullish for NVDA. Capex up with the stock down is bearish for the multiple. Capex guided flat or down for 2027 points to a trend break.
3. **Credit channel.** Watch CoreWeave bond prices and its about-Nov 10 print, and any new NVDA guarantees. Widening stress means the "who funds it" question is winning.
4. **Semi-cap guides (LRCX Oct 21, KLAC Oct 28).** Confirmation of 2027 wafer-fab-equipment growth after ASML would suggest the equipment selloff was positioning, not fundamentals.
5. **FOMC on Oct 28 with hike odds at about 54%.** A hike plus hawkish guidance on the same night as MSFT and GOOGL is the worst-case clustering for long-duration AI equities.
6. **NVDA's print.** It is not just about beating $108bn. Watch the Q4 guide (about $120bn+ is good), whether the 71–72% gross margin trough holds, the FY28 ~70% reaffirmation, and whether receivables, DSO or guarantees grow.

**Timing implication for the scenario builders**
- Catalyst density implies the bottoming *process* for semis and NVDA is more likely to resolve between **Oct 21 and Nov 20** than in the next few sessions.
- The two key nodes are the Oct 28–29 cluster and the NVDA print week (Nov 17–20, including the VIX expiration and the Nov 20 options expiration).
- A low set *before* Oct 21 would need a clear exhaustion signal, such as a TSMC-driven reversal, to be trusted.
