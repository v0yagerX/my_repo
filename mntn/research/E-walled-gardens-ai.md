# E. AI Threat 1: Walled gardens automating SMB advertising, including TV

*MNTN, Inc. (NYSE: MNTN) deep-dive. Research date: 2026-10-15. Analyst dossier: primary-source first. Every number carries a label: [reported] = company or filing figure; [guidance]; [third-party] = press, industry body or vendor data; [estimate] = my own derivation, with the math shown. Short bracketed source tags such as (S8) point to the Sources list at the end.*

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The threat is real, already under way, and growing. It is a slow squeeze, not a cliff.** All three big platforms now offer AI tools that plan, create, buy and optimize video for SMBs.
   - **Amazon** is the most dangerous for MNTN. It already sells *cross-publisher* streaming TV: Prime Video plus Netflix, Disney and Roku inventory, all through its DSP. It wraps that in a free AI Creative Agent that makes streaming-TV ads and in an Ads Agent that sets up campaigns. Its DSP page now says "DVA+ brings the power of programmatic to all advertisers" (S13, accessed 2026-10-15).
   - **Google/YouTube** already absorbs SMB "TV" spend by default:
     - Demand Gen campaigns include TV screens [reported] (S19).
     - YouTube held a record 14.2% of all US TV viewing in July 2026 [third-party] (S23).
   - **Meta** has the strongest AI automation of the three: Advantage+ is at a run-rate above $75B [reported] (S25). But Meta has **no CTV ad product today**. Its Q2-2026 call did not mention TV at all (S25). Its CTV plans are exploratory, per Digiday, Apr 27 2026 (S26).
2. **Consolidation is turning independent performance-CTV players into walled-garden features.** Three deals in seven months:
   - Pinterest agreed to buy tvScientific (Dec 11, 2025) (S41, S42).
   - Fox agreed to buy Roku for about $22B EV (Jun 15, 2026) [reported] (S32).
   - Walmart agreed to buy Vibe.co, which has more than 10,000 advertisers [reported] (Jun 23, 2026) (S40).

   After these deals, MNTN is the largest *independent*, cross-publisher, SMB-first performance-TV platform. That makes it more exposed and also more strategically valuable (an M&A target).
3. **So far the operating data show no damage.** In Q2-26:
   - Active Performance TV customers grew 40% y/y to 4,225 [reported].
   - Net expansion rate was "well north of 115%" [reported].
   - Gross margin was 80% [reported].
   - Q3-26 revenue is guided to +25% y/y at the midpoint [guidance] (S1, S4).

   One soft spot: revenue per active customer is falling, about −14% y/y [estimate, §8]. That fits a deliberate push downmarket (MNTN Express). The SMB tail is exactly where Meta, Google and Amazon AI automation is strongest.
4. **Assessment:**
   - Likelihood that walled gardens take a meaningful share of *incremental* SMB performance-TV budgets by 2030: **High**.
   - Likelihood that this materially impairs MNTN's growth or margins by 2028 to 2030: **Medium**.
   - Probability-weighted, the threat cuts about 15–20% from a "threat-contained" 2030 revenue path. A low-probability tail (Meta and Amazon both offering automated cross-publisher CTV to SMBs at scale) cuts about 40% or more [estimate, §9].
   - **Direction: this topic pushes MNTN toward "AI victim" at the margin, moderately rather than decisively.**

**Q3-2026 status:** not yet released. MNTN said it will report Q3-2026 results after market close on **Wednesday, November 4, 2026** [reported] (S7; press-release date Oct 14, 2026, seen via search result; the page itself returned 403 to my fetch). Nothing in this dossier depends on Q3 actuals.

## 2. Player-by-player evidence

### 2.1 Amazon: the most direct threat

- **AI creation for TV.** At unBoxed (Nov 11, 2025), Amazon said Creative Agent "can now create streaming TV ads" (S10).
  - PPC Land reports the tool is free and covers Prime Video, Twitch, Fire TV apps and third-party streaming. It is available in North America to vendors, sellers, managed-service and self-service advertisers (S11).
- **AI planning and buying.**
  - unBoxed also launched Ads Agent: "accessible in a conversational, chat window… throughout Amazon Ads."
  - It also launched Full-Funnel Campaigns, described as "Our first AI-powered full-funnel campaign type", across Sponsored Products, display and streaming TV (S10).
  - At CES 2026, Amazon said Full-Funnel Campaigns launch in Q1-2026, start with streaming TV, and are set up in natural language (S12).
  - Amazon's DSP product page now reads: "Amazon DSP is now part of Amazon Ads Agent" and "DVA+ brings the power of programmatic to all advertisers with simplified, outcome-based campaign creation" (S13, accessed 2026-10-15; the page gives no transition date).
- **Inventory breadth.** This is what makes Amazon different from the other walled gardens.
  - Amazon–Roku (Jun 2025): an estimated 80M US CTV households, more than 80% of the market [third-party, company-cited ComScore] (S14). Paul Kotas: "The collaboration enables agencies and brands that use Amazon DSP to benefit from greater efficiency and higher performance" (S14).
  - Netflix inventory through Amazon DSP in 11 markets from Q4-2025 (S15).
  - CES 2026: "premium audio and video streaming inventory from Netflix, Disney, Spotify, and others through Amazon DSP" (S12).
  - Amazon claims its authenticated graph "connects advertisers to 90% of all U.S. households deterministically" [reported, company claim] (S10).
- **SMB entry point.**
  - Sponsored TV has "no minimum campaign spend, no minimum daily spend, and no upfront commitments." But it is only for brands that sell on Amazon, and it reports Amazon-store outcomes such as branded searches and detail-page views (S16, UK launch Aug 21, 2024).
  - Several agency blogs claim Amazon removed the self-service DSP minimum at unBoxed 2025. **I could not verify this in any Amazon or trade-press primary source. Treat it as unconfirmed.**
- **Scale and results (Q2-26, Jul 30, 2026).**
  - Ads revenue $19.8B, +26% y/y [reported] (S8, S9).
  - Jassy: "Advertisers using Ads Agent targeting see 8% lower cost per impression and 6% lower cost per acquisition" [reported, company claim] (S8).
  - Thursday Night Football, NBA, WNBA and NASCAR inventory "all sold out" (S8).
  - Amazon does not disclose an SMB TV advertiser count.
- **What Amazon cannot do for an SMB.**
  - Measurement is Amazon-graded and centered on Amazon retail signals.
  - Its natural customer base is Amazon sellers. MNTN's base includes many DTC, services, health, finance and B2B advertisers with no Amazon storefront (S3, S4).
  - Amazon does not sell YouTube, and Prime Video is first-party.

### 2.2 Google / YouTube: the default absorber of SMB "TV" budgets

- **Watch-time share.** Nielsen's July 2026 Gauge (Sep 10, 2026): YouTube rose to "a record 14.2%" of US TV viewing, and its lead in the Media Distributor Gauge widened to 5.0 share points over No. 2 NBCU-Versant (9.2%) [third-party] (S23).
  - July 2025 was 13.4% [third-party, via Adwave citing Nielsen] (S24). That is about +0.8pt y/y [estimate].
- **Q2-26 call quotes (Philipp Schindler, CBO; third-party transcript, S18):**
  - "In the living room, we see continued momentum across both brand and direct response."
  - "SMBs continue to fuel our direct response growth through campaigns like Demand Gen."
  - "over half of our SMB customers globally use AI to create or optimize their creatives."
  - "our new agentic solutions for SMBs have expanded our reach by hundreds of thousands of new customers year to date."
  - AI Max "is out of beta, and half a million advertisers have already adopted it."
  - Buy with Google Pay lets viewers buy on their TVs, "turning the TV screen into a stronger performance surface."
  - YouTube ads revenue was $11.1B, +13% [reported].
- **Product mechanics.**
  - Google's Jan 22, 2026 Demand Gen update says Demand Gen "powers Shoppable CTV" and that "Demand Gen campaigns that include TV screens drive an average of 7% additional conversions at the same ROI." That figure is Google's own internal experiment [reported, vendor claim] (S19).
  - Google Ads device targeting lists "TV screens" for Display and Video campaigns (S22).
  - In practice, any SMB that lets Google's AI allocate a video budget is *already* buying CTV on YouTube. It does not have to decide to "buy TV".
- **What Google cannot do for an SMB.** Self-serve Google Ads reaches YouTube (plus Google video partners), not Hulu, Paramount+, HBO Max or ESPN. DV360 reaches those but is enterprise and agency tooling. Measurement is Google's own.

### 2.3 Meta: the most automated, but not in TV (yet)

- **Automation scale (Q2-26 call, Jul 29, 2026; S25, verbatim):**
  - "Our AI-powered Advantage+ end-to-end solutions continued to grow, reaching over $75 billion in annual revenue run-rate this quarter."
  - "Adoption of our Gen AI ad creative tools continues to scale, with over 9 million small businesses using at least one AI creative tool."
  - Image-generation adoption "more than double[d] this quarter" [reported].
- **Full-automation goal.**
  - WSJ (reported Jun 2–3, 2025): Meta aims to let advertisers supply only a product image and a budget by end-2026. AI would generate the creative, pick audiences on Facebook and Instagram, and suggest budgets (S27).
  - Zuckerberg framed the goal as a business that will "tell us what your objective is" and needs no creative or targeting of its own (paraphrased and partly quoted via S27, a secondary source).
  - **I found no confirmation that the end-2026 goal has been met.**
- **TV.**
  - The Q2-26 transcript contains **zero** mentions of "TV", "CTV" or "television" (my text search of S25).
  - Digiday (Apr 27, 2026) reports exploratory meetings since early 2025 with Magnite, FreeWheel, TV makers and publishers. The likely end state is "audience extension" for CTV. Sources say plans "remain fluid", Meta is "still in planning mode", and "They need the pipes" (S26).
  - The same piece frames this as a way to recapture budgets moving to performance-CTV players such as tvScientific and Vibe (S26).
- **What Meta cannot do today:** buy any premium TV inventory.
- **Why it matters most as a tail risk:** if Meta launches CTV audience extension, Advantage+ could allocate SMB budgets into TV automatically. Meta's SMB base (9M+ using AI creative alone) is about 2,000 times MNTN's 4,225 active customers [estimate: 9,000,000 / 4,225 ≈ 2,130].

### 2.4 Roku: SMB self-serve scaling, now heading into Fox

- **Q4-25 letter (Feb 12, 2026):**
  - Ads Manager is "our robust self-serve platform for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) and performance advertisers."
  - Campaigns move from "create campaign" to "publish" "in under five minutes."
  - "we are investing in Roku Ads Manager to capture a vast opportunity" (S30).
- **On the call (S31):**
  - CEO Anthony Wood: "the easier it is to create video ads… the larger the number of advertisers that can advertise on a TV platform."
  - CFO Dan Jedda: "You should not think that, as we focus on SMBs, it drags down margins."
  - AdExchanger noted that Roku's approach "follows MNTN's playbook" (S31).
- **Q1-26 letter (Apr 30, 2026):**
  - "The number of advertisers using Ads Manager in Q1 more than doubled YoY" [reported].
  - Roku says it targets "a meaningful share of the approximately $600 billion in annual SMB ad spend" [reported, company estimate] (S29).
- **Q2-26 letter (Aug 6, 2026):**
  - Ad revenue $673M, +25% [reported].
  - Smartly connected to Ads Manager via the Roku Ads API, "expected to add incremental performance revenue by early 2027" (S28).
  - No earnings call was held because of the pending Fox deal.
- **Fox deal (Jun 15, 2026):** $160/share, about $22B EV [reported], expected to close 1H CY2027 (S32).
- **Implication for MNTN:** Fox, Tubi and Roku become one vertically integrated seller with its own SMB front door. That is a channel conflict for MNTN, which depends on direct deals with networks.

### 2.5 Netflix: not an SMB self-serve threat

- **Q2-26 letter (Jul 16, 2026):**
  - "we remain on track to deliver approximately $3 billion in ads revenue in 2026" [guidance].
  - "In Q2, we expanded our AI-powered tools across the full advertising lifecycle, from planning and creative production…"
  - Extending programmatic access "reduces the manual effort that has historically limited access for smaller buyers" (S33).
- **Programmatic and advertisers:** programmatic is approaching 50% of non-live inventory. DSP partners are TTD, DV360, Microsoft, Yahoo and Amazon. Over 4,000 advertisers [third-party summaries of Q1-26 and the May 13, 2026 upfront] (S34).
- **No self-serve SMB product exists.** For SMBs, Netflix is reachable *through Amazon DSP*, which raises the Amazon threat, or through MNTN-style intermediaries.

### 2.6 Disney: self-serve, Disney-only inventory

- Disney Campaign Manager had onboarded 4,200 brands and 1,000 agencies, with $500-per-unit buys, as of Jan 10, 2024 [reported, stale] (S35).
- Ad Creative Studio (closed beta from Jul 23, 2026) "unlocks creative access to connected TV for small and mid-sized businesses" (S36).
- Disney's Real-Time Ad Exchange (DRAX) is also open to Amazon DSP (S12 lists Disney inventory via Amazon DSP).

### 2.7 Comcast Universal Ads: the closest "media-owner" analogue to MNTN

- **Traction:** undisclosed. Digiday notes small brands still split roughly 70/30 in favor of social and search [third-party, one agency's estimate] (S38).

### 2.8 Walmart / VIZIO + Vibe.co

### 2.9 Samsung, LG

- **LG:** Agentiv (Oct 30, 2025) has more than 20 agents, mostly internal. A limited advertiser beta starts with a data-collaboration agent. No SMB self-serve (S43).
- **Samsung Ads:** I found no SMB self-serve or agentic product. **Data gap.**

**Conclusion:**
- **Amazon** is the only walled garden that today closes most of the loop *with cross-publisher premium TV*. Its weak spots are neutrality and fit for non-Amazon sellers.
- **Google** closes the whole loop, but only for YouTube.
- **Meta** cannot sell TV at all yet.

The common belief that "walled gardens can't buy each other's inventory" is **true for Google and Meta and largely false for Amazon** (S12, S14, S15).

## 4. Evidence FOR the threat

1. **Default allocation.**
   - SMBs already run Meta, Google and Amazon pixels, payments and creative. AI agents turn TV into a checkbox: Demand Gen includes TV screens; Amazon Full-Funnel starts with streaming TV (S12, S19).
   - Google says it has added "hundreds of thousands of new [SMB] customers" through agentic tools this year [reported] (S18).
2. **YouTube is the largest TV "network".** It had 14.2% of US TV time in July 2026, a record [third-party] (S23). For many SMBs, "TV" increasingly means YouTube on the TV set.
3. **Amazon's cross-publisher reach plus free AI creative plus an outcome-based DSP for "all advertisers"** (S10–S15) directly copies MNTN's value proposition, with first-party purchase data MNTN lacks.
4. **Media owners are building their own SMB front doors:**
   - Roku Ads Manager advertisers more than doubled y/y [reported] (S29).
   - Universal Ads added linear inventory and MCP agent access (S38).
   - Disney launched an SMB AI creative studio (S36).
   - Netflix is widening programmatic access to "smaller buyers" (S33).
5. **Platforms are buying the category.** Pinterest–tvScientific, Fox–Roku and Walmart–Vibe (S32, S40, S41). Fox–Roku also puts a major supplier and a competing SMB self-serve tool under one owner.
6. **AI commoditizes MNTN's creative wedge.** About 95% of MNTN customers had never advertised on TV [reported] (S3), so creative was a key barrier. Free AI TV-ad creation is now table stakes at Amazon, Google, Disney, Comcast and Roku's partners.
7. **MNTN's own downmarket mix shift.** TTM customers grew +40% while Q2 revenue grew +21%, implying lower revenue per customer [estimate, §8]. Small advertisers have the lowest switching costs and are the most "default-to-platform".

## 5. Evidence AGAINST (or limiting) the threat

1. **MNTN's numbers are still accelerating** (S1, S4):
   - Customers +40% y/y [reported].
   - Expansion rate above 115% [reported].
   - Q3-26 revenue guided at +25% y/y midpoint [guidance].
   - Gross margin 80% [reported].
   - In Feb 2026 Douglas said: "I don't think a lot of companies are running into each other at this point" (S5).
2. **Meta has no CTV supply.** Its Q2-26 call did not mention TV (S25), and Digiday describes "planning mode" (S26).
3. **Google's SMB TV offer is YouTube-only.** Premium long-form content (sports, Bravo, HBO Max, ESPN) is MNTN's pitch: "hundreds of premium streaming TV networks like Paramount Plus, Disney, and ESPN" (S5). Live-sports access was highlighted on the Q2 call (S4).
4. **Networks have a strategic reason to keep a neutral SMB channel.**
   - Douglas: networks "view us as a growth channel" (S6).
   - Over 99% of MNTN's inventory is bought through private-marketplace (PMP) deals at pre-negotiated prices (S4).
   - Paramount, WBD, NBCU and Disney have little reason to let Amazon or Google own their SMB demand. But NBCU built Universal Ads, which cuts the other way.
5. **SMBs historically never bought TV through walled gardens.**
   - About 95% of MNTN customers are new to TV [reported] (S3).
   - Douglas: "not a penny of it was going to television without our platform" (S6).
   - Walled-garden TV products are young: Sponsored TV dates from 2023, Universal Ads from 2025, Creative Agent's TV support from Nov 2025.
6. **Measurement neutrality and portability.** MNTN pushes conversion data to about 11 attribution platforms (S5) and offers "one cost… the cost of media" with no add-on fees (S4). Walled gardens grade their own homework.
7. **Scale asymmetry cuts both ways.** MNTN's Q2-26 revenue was about 0.4% of Amazon's Q2 ad revenue and about 0.7% of YouTube's [estimate: 82.5/19,809 and 82.5/11,100]. MNTN is too small to be a priority target, and it can grow a lot without walled gardens noticing.

## 6. Why would an SMB use MNTN instead of letting a walled garden allocate its budget?

**What MNTN offers:**
1. **Premium, cross-publisher TV in one buy.**
   - Google Ads reaches only YouTube; Meta reaches no TV; Roku, Disney and Netflix reach only their own inventory; Universal Ads excludes Netflix, Disney, Amazon and YouTube (S37, S38).
   - MNTN has direct PMP deals with "nearly, if not all" ad-supported US media companies, including live sports (S4).
   - An SMB that wants ESPN, Bravo and HBO Max next to each other has few alternatives. Amazon DSP is the main one.
2. **Performance measured on the SMB's own site and CRM, and portable.** Household cross-device attribution (Verified Visits) is exported to Google Analytics, Northbeam, HubSpot and about 11 MMP/MTA tools (S4, S5). That lets the SMB compare TV against Meta and Google on a common scale.
3. **TV that is not owned by the platform the SMB is trying to diversify away from.** Ad spend on MNTN is incremental reach. It is not the same algorithm that already governs the SMB's social and search budget.
4. **Packaging built for SMBs.** Express, Pro and Premium tiers. Free QuickFrame AI creative. Single media price with no add-on fees. Pre-negotiated CPMs insulated from political and Q4 spikes (S4).

**Where this answer gets weaker:**
- Amazon now offers points 1, 2 (partly) and 4.
- For the smallest advertisers (MNTN Express), "good-enough TV" on YouTube via Demand Gen, at Google's scale and with no extra vendor, is a strong substitute.

So MNTN's moat is strongest for *mid-market, non-Amazon-seller* brands that value premium long-form and sports content and want measurement they can audit. It is weakest for micro-SMBs and Amazon-native sellers.
