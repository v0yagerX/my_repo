## 1. Bottom line

1. **Between Dec 2025 and Jun 2026 the competitive set changed character.** The two venture-backed pure plays most similar to MNTN were bought by data-rich giants:
   - **Walmart / Vibe.co.** Announced 2026-06-23 and closed 2026-08-04. WSJ reported about $1.2B cash plus about $180M in executive retention [third-party].
   - **Pinterest / tvScientific.** Closed 2026-02-17 for $465.1M in total purchase consideration [reported].

   At the same time:
   - **Fox agreed to buy Roku** for about $22B EV [reported]. Roku owns the Roku Ads Manager self-serve tool.
   - **Comcast's Universal Ads** added no-minimum self-serve linear TV, AI video generation and an MCP agent interface [third-party/company].

   MNTN's main competitors are now trillion-dollar and multi-billion-dollar platforms that own first-party data. Two-person teams with AI coding tools are not the main threat.
2. **AI has lowered barriers in software, UI and creative.** Self-serve buying tools and AI ad-generators are now common:
   - Universal Ads, Roku, Disney, Paramount, Tubi, Amazon, Vibe and Adwave all offer self-serve buying.
   - Waymark, Universal Ads' AI video generator, Vibe Studio, Adwave Wavemaker and MNTN's own free QuickFrame AI all generate ads.

   AI has **not** lowered the binding barriers:
   - negotiated premium supply (MNTN says it is ">99%" private-marketplace [reported, Q2'26 call]);
   - attribution data and credible measurement;
   - distribution and customer acquisition. MNTN spends about 35% of revenue on S&M [reported].

   The best evidence is that the winners took 4-5 years and tens of millions of dollars to build, and then sold to owners of data and distribution. A $1M pre-seed AI-native entrant (Adwave) shows no measurable traction.
3. **There is no direct evidence of MNTN losing customers.** There is circumstantial evidence of losing *share of growth* at the margin:
   - Northbeam's tracked-spend data for H1'26 vs H1'25 reportedly showed **Vibe +1,900% vs MNTN +29%** [third-party, low-to-medium confidence].
   - MNTN's ex-Maximum-Effort revenue growth slowed from 46% (Q1'25) to 21% (Q2'26) [reported].
   - TTM revenue per active customer fell about 9% y/y [estimate].

   Offsetting evidence:
   - customer count still +40% y/y;
   - expansion rate "well north of 115%" (management);
   - Q3'26 guidance implies about 25% growth, a reacceleration [guidance].
4. **Thesis implication: modest push toward "AI victim" through 2027, larger by 2028-2030 (medium likelihood).** The risk is not a flood of cheap AI clones. It is three things together:
   - (a) walled gardens (Walmart, Amazon, Pinterest, Roku/Fox, Comcast, possibly AppLovin later) bundling performance CTV with first-party commerce data and closed-loop measurement;
   - (b) agentic/MCP buying commoditizing MNTN's ease-of-use edge;
   - (c) CPM deflation and fee competition.

   M&A prices are a counterweight. Walmart and Pinterest paid roughly 2x MNTN's whole EV for Vibe, and about 9x net revenue for tvScientific [estimate]. That supports strategic value and takeout optionality.

### 3a. The four that matter most

**Walmart Connect + Vibe.co (High).** This is the most direct substitute for MNTN.
- **Same pitch and customer.** Vibe uses the same "as easy as search/social" pitch to the same e-commerce/SMB buyer.
- **Lower entry price.** Entry is $50/day with no commitment [company].
- **Agent-ready.** Vibe is ahead on agentic buying. Its MCP integration is used by 1,000+ brands, and its CEO says he "would not use a new platform without an MCP" [third-party, AdExchanger 2026-10-08].
- **What Walmart adds.** First-party purchase data on 150M+ customers [company FAQ], Vizio ACR data and closed-loop sales measurement. Walmart can also cross-sell to marketplace sellers. MNTN cannot match that targeting and measurement for retail-sold brands.
- **Mitigants.**
  - Walmart has not committed to keeping Vibe neutral or open to non-Walmart advertisers [third-party, Edge226; Walmart release speaks of an "open, collaborative ecosystem"].
  - Integration usually distracts; the founders face 4-year retention lock-ups.
  - Publishers may see a retailer-owned buyer as a channel-conflict risk. Next in Media asked whether Vibe will be confined to "lesser inventory" [third-party, Mike Shields, 2026-07-14, https://mikeshields.substack.com/p/why-paramount-and-maybe-others-are].
  - A "walled garden" may not appeal to DTC brands that do not sell at Walmart [third-party, Cynopsis 2026-08-11, https://www.cynopsis.com/streamlined-8-11-2026-walmart-catches-a-vibe/].

**Pinterest + tvScientific (Med-High).**
- **Pricing.** tvScientific prices on a cost-per-outcome basis, a sharper risk-transfer offer than MNTN's dCPM.
- **Pinterest's ambitions.** Pinterest wants to "extend our AI-powered performance advertising from mobile to TV" [reported, 10-Q] and to fold tvScientific into Performance+ [third-party].
- **Scale today.** It is smaller than MNTN, at roughly $50M net revenue [third-party]. Its go-to-market is demo-led and skews to larger performance brands.
- **Biggest risk.** If Pinterest makes CTV a checkbox inside its self-serve Performance+ for its advertiser base, that becomes a low-CAC distribution channel MNTN lacks.

**Comcast Universal Ads (Med-High).** This is the cleanest example of AI-driven barrier collapse:
- no minimum spend;
- an AI video generator;
- a URL-to-ad tool;
- an MCP connector;
- NBCU/Paramount/Fox/WBD/Roku supply in one interface;
- linear TV added in Apr-2026.

Limits: measurement is still being built, with 8 audience/MMP partners added 2026-07-30 [third-party]. Its SMB proof points are small, e.g. 20 pizzerias at about $500/month showing a 3.2% incremental sales lift [third-party, Digiday]. Ownership is uncertain after Comcast's spin-off.

**Amazon (Med-High for Amazon sellers).**
- Self-serve Sponsored/Streaming TV has no minimum for Brand-Registry sellers [third-party].
- Since Q4'25, Amazon DSP has Roku supply and exclusive third-party access to Roku data [third-party].
- Amazon's closed-loop retail attribution is very hard for MNTN to match among brands that sell on Amazon.

## 4. Has AI coding actually lowered barriers? What is hard to copy

| Barrier | Did AI lower it? | Evidence | Durability of MNTN's edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campaign UI / self-serve software** | **Yes, largely** | At least 10 self-serve CTV UIs exist (Universal Ads, Roku, Disney, Paramount, Tubi, Amazon, Vibe, Skybeam, Adwave, AdRoll). Tubi's tool is *built on* Universal Ads tech [reported, Fox]. A $1M-pre-seed company (Adwave) ships a multichannel + TV product at $30/day [company]. | **Low.** Agentic/MCP buying (Vibe, Universal Ads) further commoditizes "ease of use", MNTN's original pitch. |
| **Creative production** | **Yes, collapsed** | Free or near-free AI TV-ad generation: Universal Ads, Waymark (in Hulu/Roku/Paramount tools), Vibe Studio, Adwave, Starti. MNTN made QuickFrame AI free: 37K signups in Q2'26, 73K+ YTD; monetization "under review" [reported, Q2'26 call]. | **Low** as a moat, though useful as a top-of-funnel lead magnet. |
| **Premium supply access (PMPs / direct deals)** | **No** | MNTN: ">99% private marketplace deals" and direct deals with "nearly all" US ad-supported media companies [reported, Q2'26 call]; "150+" networks [company site]. Premium live sports and tentpoles are often kept off open programmatic; Paramount: "Not every streaming experience should be bought the same way" [third-party, Shields 2026-07-14]. MNTN placed mid-sized advertisers in March Madness, Love Island and the World Cup via pre-negotiated deals [third-party]. | **Medium-High**, but not exclusive. Universal Ads and Amazon own or aggregate supply directly, and Vibe and tvScientific connect to most SSPs. |
| **Attribution data / models** | **Partially** | MNTN Matched and Verified Visits rely on years of outcome data across thousands of advertisers. Rivals hold *better* first-party data: Walmart purchase data (150M+ customers), Amazon shopper data, Pinterest intent signals (600M MAU). | **Medium** vs startups; **Low** vs walled gardens. |
| **Measurement credibility** | **No** (if anything harder) | MNTN added independent verification: Northbeam (Dec-2025), Upwave, HubSpot [reported]. Closed-loop retail measurement (Walmart, Amazon) is structurally stronger for retail-sold goods. | **Medium.** It is a real asset for DTC/e-commerce. |
| **Brand, onboarding and sales (CAC)** | **No** | MNTN S&M $28.6M = 34.7% of Q2'26 revenue (Q2'25: 35.5%) [reported; ratio = estimate]. "95% of our customers have never advertised on TV before" [reported, 10-K and AdExchanger], so every customer must be educated. AppLovin's CEO admits an "awareness problem" when selling to new long-tail advertisers [reported, Q2'26 call]. | **Medium-High** against startups. Walled gardens get CAC almost free from existing advertiser bases. |
| **Working capital (pay publishers before collecting from SMBs)** | **Low barrier anyway** | MNTN bills gross with mostly 30-day terms. At Q2'26, AR was $60.0M vs AP and accrued expenses of $53.8M, roughly self-funding [reported]. Startups avoid the issue with prepay or credit cards (Vibe: "Credit Card or Wire"; Adwave: prepaid wallet) [company]. | **Low** moat. |
| **Fraud / brand safety** | **No** | MNTN's PMP-only model ("We don't buy open market inventory" [third-party, AdExchanger 2026-08-07]) limits CTV-fraud exposure. Platforms with broad SSP access face more verification burden [qualitative]. | **Medium.** It matters more as low-cost entrants scale. |

**Verdict.** AI coding has commoditized the *software and creative layers*, which was much of MNTN's 2018-2023 differentiation. It has not commoditized supply, outcome data, measurement trust or distribution.

The market's actual structure shows this:
- The two scaled startups each needed about 4-5 years. Vibe raised about $80M ($7.5M seed + $22.5M A + $50M B) [third-party].
- Both then sold to companies that bring data and distribution.

The AI threat to MNTN is therefore not "1,000 cheap clones". It is that AI lets walled gardens and publishers bolt a "good-enough" self-serve performance-TV product onto existing advertiser bases at near-zero marginal CAC. Agentic buying then makes the interface irrelevant, so competition shifts to measured outcomes per dollar.

## 7. Likelihood, timing and impact

| Threat vector | Likelihood | Timing | Impact on MNTN if it materializes [estimate] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walmart/Vibe pricing and data-bundling offensive in mid-market DTC | High (55-70%) | 2027-2028, after integration | Revenue growth −2 to −5 pts/yr vs. otherwise; pressure on mid-market win rates and dCPM pricing |

| Amazon / Google / Pinterest bundling CTV into existing self-serve performance suites | Medium (35-50%) | 2027-2030 | The largest long-run risk: near-zero-CAC distribution to millions of SMB advertisers |
| AppLovin entering CTV | Low near term (≤20% by 2027); Medium by 2030 | CEO calls CTV "step three" | High if it happens. Axon's e-commerce ROAS plus Wurl supply would directly attack MNTN's DTC base |

**Through 2030 (illustrative, for the model) [estimate]:**
- **Base case:** MNTN remains the largest independent performance-TV platform. Growth fades from ~24% (FY26 guidance) to mid-teens by 2028-2030 as walled gardens take the incremental SMB dollar, with modest (0-3 pt) gross-margin/take-rate erosion.
- **Bear case:** the Walmart/Amazon/Pinterest bundles plus agentic buying force fee cuts and slow growth to high-single-digit/low-teens by 2028, with S&M intensity stuck ≥33%.
- **Bull case:** consolidation removes independent rivals. Publishers favour a neutral, non-retailer buyer to avoid channel conflict. MNTN's PMP supply and DTC measurement keep growth at 20%+, with M&A optionality at a strategic multiple (§5).

## 8. Thesis implications

- **Direction: modest push toward "AI victim"** on competition, not toward existential risk. The AI-enabled threat is real at the software, creative and interface layer, which used to be MNTN's moat. The defensible assets are supply relationships, outcome data, measurement integrations and a public-company balance sheet ($237.3M cash, no debt [reported]). These are not AI-replicable, but they are matchable by Walmart, Amazon, Pinterest, Comcast and Roku/Fox.
- **What would move the needle:**
  - (1) Q3'26 (Nov 4) and Q4'26 customer-count growth and revenue per customer. Watch whether net adds hold at ≥300/quarter.
  - (2) Any MNTN disclosure on win rates, Express conversion or take rate.
  - (3) Whether Walmart keeps Vibe open to non-Walmart advertisers and at $50/day.
  - (4) Whether AppLovin or Google move performance CTV into their self-serve suites.
  - (5) Universal Ads' post-spin owner and publisher roster.
- **Offset:** M&A comps (Vibe ~$1.2-1.4B; tvScientific $465.1M at ~8-9x net revenue [estimate]) imply that the market-implied EV (~$520-570M, ~1.5x revenue) already discounts heavy competitive damage. That favours an asymmetric outcome if growth holds in the high-teens or better.
