# Pre-mortem #1: MNTN at about $3 in December 2030 (down about 70% from $10.25)

**Bottom line:** I put the probability of this outcome at about **10%** (range 7–13%). A $3 stock needs more than the model's bear case. The business has to shrink **and** management has to spend or dilute away most of today's ~$3.17 per share of net cash.

Everything dated after 2026-10-15 below is a made-up scenario, and all 2026Q4–2030 numbers are [estimate]. Bracketed tags cite the evidence as of today.

**Why $3 is hard to reach.** Today net cash is $237.3M and there is no debt, about $3.17 per share [reported; A/K, 10-Q Q2'26]. The model's bear case values the stock at about $5–8 [estimate; 00-model-output-v1]. In that case:
- 2030 revenue is $290M.
- Adjusted EBITDA is $55M.
- Cash grows to about $324M.

So $3 requires bear-case operations *and* the loss of most of the cash cushion. The story below has both.

## The causal story

**Nov 2026–2027: the platforms take the funnel.** The crack opened on Nov 4, 2026. Q3 landed inside the $86–89M guide, but the Q4 guide implied about $102M against $108.4M consensus [consensus; K, Yahoo]. Quarterly-active customers grew only about 16%, continuing the slide from +85% to +24% [reported; C, 10-Q Q2'26]. The stock fell about 25%, as it had (−23%) on the May 2026 guide miss [third-party; K]. The FY27 guide of about +12% missed the $417M consensus [consensus; K].

Management blamed the go-to-market rebuild again. The real cause was the one its own 10-K names: customers and sellers transacting "directly ... or through intermediaries other than us" [reported; A, 10-K FY25].
- Amazon's DVA+ and Full-Funnel Campaigns moved small sellers into streaming TV by default [third-party; H, AdExchanger 2026-09-29].
- Walmart folded Vibe ($50/day minimum; AI-agent buying used by 1,000+ brands) into Walmart Connect [third-party; G, F].
- Roku Ads Manager (under Fox from 1H27) and Pinterest/tvScientific did the same for their own users [reported; E, G].

Those routes caught the new-to-TV small businesses, who are 95–97% of MNTN's customers [reported; C]. QuickFrame AI kept adding about 35K signups a quarter but converted only about 1–2%, as the 1H26 arithmetic implied [estimate; I]. Express-tier small businesses churned; the CEO puts typical small-business spend at about $15K a year [third-party transcript; B]. The flattering trailing-twelve-month (TTM) customer count peaked near 5,100 in 2027.

**2H27–1H28: an SMB ad recession hits a base with no contracts.** 2026 ad spending had been inflated by the Olympics, the World Cup and the midterms [third-party; J]. With CPI re-accelerating (+3.7%) and NFIB uncertainty at 90 [reported; J, NFIB/FRED], small businesses cut budgets. MNTN was exposed on three counts [reported; J, 424B4/10-K]:
- About 91–92% of revenue came from small and mid-sized businesses.
- Almost no customers had contracts.
- Q4 was about 30% of the year.

Q4'27 revenue fell year on year for the first time. That was milder than Yelp's −32% in 2020 [reported; J], but this time there was no V-shaped recovery: returning advertisers came back through Amazon and Walmart instead.

**2028: the take rate breaks.** MNTN's undisclosed cut of customers' gross media spend, about 35–50% [estimate; A], became the target. Three things exposed it:
1. AI buying agents compared cost per acquisition against Amazon's reported 0–1% buying-platform fees and Google's ~10% fee cap [third-party; H].
2. MNTN's own agency push (agency-led accounts up about 4x) brought in buyers who expect fee transparency [reported; C].
3. After the FTC's August 2026 suit against Amazon over opaque small-business ad pricing [third-party; H], the Trustpilot complaint that MNTN "mark[s] up the media cost" [M] became a regulatory question.

MNTN cut its effective take rate about 7 points. At roughly 2% of revenue per point [estimate; H], FY28 revenue fell about 13% even though gross spend was flat. Meta then launched TV ad buying ("audience extension") inside Advantage+. Dossier E had flagged this as a low-to-medium likelihood, high-impact tail risk [third-party; E, Digiday]. It closed off the mid-market as a fallback.

**2028–29: attribution loses its authority.** Verified Visits credits MNTN when anyone in the household visits the site within 7 or 14 days of a view, and it blends in modeled outcomes [reported; M]. MNTN's own 10% holdout tests and its Haus and Northbeam integrations [M] gave customers' finance teams clean reads of true incremental lift. Those reads came in well below the self-reported "2–7x" lifts [reported; N]. Ari Paparo's "correlation ≠ causation" critique became the common view [third-party; M]. A class action over its tracking pixel (under the CIPA and VPPA privacy laws), a risk the 10-K already lists [reported; A], froze upsells.

**What management got wrong:**
1. **It read the wrong metric.** It steered by TTM customers (+40%) while quarterly-active growth decayed. It never disclosed take rate, net revenue retention or cohorts [C], so when growth broke, investors assumed the worst and were right.
2. **It defended the spread, then cut in a panic.** It held the opaque spread through 2027, when trading margin for distribution through AI agents was still cheap. Vibe shipped a read-write agent interface (MCP) first, MNTN's stayed in beta, and MNTN was missing from Adobe's activation list [F, N].
3. **It kept churning sales and marketing.** S&M stayed above 30% of revenue through repeated hire-cut-rehire cycles [M], even as incremental revenue per S&M dollar fell from 0.85x to 0.72x [estimate; C].
4. **It spent the cash.**
   - It finished the $100M buyback [reported; L] at about $8–9 a share, roughly 3x the 2030 price.
   - In 2028 it paid about $120M for a startup in agentic buying and incrementality measurement. CEO options vest only above $25.40, which rewards big bets [reported; L].
   - That goodwill and the existing $51.9M [reported; A] were written off in 2029.

**What else made it worse (not AI):**
- **Overhang.** Pre-IPO holders owned about 44% of shares against average daily volume of 0.7–1.0M shares [estimate; L]. They sold into every rally, as Qualcomm had in 2026.
- **Control.** Class B shares held about 66% of the vote until 2032 [reported; L], which blocked activists. The window for a sale of the company closed unused, even after Walmart paid about 2x MNTN's enterprise value for Vibe [G].
- **Dilution.** Stock comp of about $25M a year [A] was issued at $3–5 a share. Add the full 5% + 1% evergreen share reserve [reported; L] and a Trade Desk-style option repricing [O], and the share count rose back above 80M.

## Probability: about 10% (range 7–13%)

- **Starting point.** Revenue actually declining in 2028–30, from walled-garden share capture plus a take-rate reset: about 25%. That is the model's 30% bear case less the bear paths where revenue stabilizes near $290M.
- **Cash cushion lost.** Given that, the chance the net-cash cushion is largely spent or diluted away (an acquisition, buybacks at higher prices, losses, stock comp at low prices) is about 35%. That gives about 9%.
- **No takeout.** Given that, the chance no strategic buyer rescues the price is about 85%. The dual-class vote can block a sale, though precedents (Vibe at ~2x MNTN's enterprise value; tvScientific at ~8–9x net revenue [G, O]) suggest buyers exist. That gives about 7.5%.
- **Other routes.** Add about 2–3% for paths not in this chain: a deep 2027–28 small-business recession with lasting impairment, or a stand-alone attribution or pricing scandal. That gives about 10%.

What argues against this outcome today: the $3.17 per share of net cash, a 9–10% FY26E free-cash-flow yield [estimate; K], and no visible take-rate compression yet: receivables per $ of revenue have fallen, gross margin is 80%, and concessions are flat [H]. This scenario is a left tail that needs management to make capital-allocation mistakes, not just competition to arrive.
