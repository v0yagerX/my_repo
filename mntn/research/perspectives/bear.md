# MNTN: Short. The last independent in a category the platforms are absorbing

**$10.25 | Target $5–7 [estimate] | Horizon 4–8 quarters**

**Thesis.** MNTN sold first-time TV advertisers three things: a TV-quality ad, an easy self-serve purchase, and a performance number. For that it keeps roughly 35–50% of media spend, a share it does not disclose [A; estimate]. AI has made the first two free, and the big platforms now give away the third alongside their own customer data. What remains is a reseller of other companies' inventory. It runs on demand-side platforms (DSPs) it rents [A; 10-K, reported], and its growth has halved. The low multiple is deserved.

## 1. Growth is slowing faster than the headline shows
- Growth excluding Maximum Effort (ex-ME) fell from 46% (Q1'25) to 21% (Q2'26) [A; reported].
- The +40% trailing-twelve-month (TTM) customer count includes paid trials. Customers active in the quarter grew only +24%, against +85% a year earlier [C; 10-Q, reported].
- Revenue per TTM customer fell from ~$106K (FY23) to $74.2K, down 8.7% y/y. Each net new customer brings 0.76x the average [C; estimate].
- Spend per customer has stopped falling only because new-customer growth slowed. The Express small-business tier will push it down again: the CEO puts small-business spend at ~$15K a year [B; reported].
- QuickFrame AI had 73K signups [reported], but TTM customers rose by only 593. Conversion is therefore at most ~1–3% [I; estimate].
- Incremental revenue per sales-and-marketing (S&M) dollar fell from 0.85x to 0.72x [C; estimate].
- The FY26 guide needs ~24.6% growth in 2H, against ~21.1% underlying growth in 1H [B; estimate]. MNTN lands on average only +0.4% above its guidance midpoint [B; estimate, corrected], so there is no cushion.

## 2. The big platforms and AI agents have taken the moat
- **Consolidation:**
  - Pinterest closed tvScientific for $465.1M, and Walmart closed Vibe (10,000+ advertisers, $50/day minimum) [G; reported/third-party].
  - Fox–Roku closes 1H27, and Roku Ads Manager advertisers have more than doubled [E; reported].
  - Paramount–WBD closed Oct 6 [D; reported].
  - MNTN's suppliers are merging and building their own small-business front doors.
- **Amazon:** Amazon and Roku together reach ~80M households, and Amazon adds Netflix and Disney [E; third-party, corrected]. Amazon also offers a free Creative Agent that makes streaming-TV ads. YouTube and Amazon hold 18.5% of TV time, and there is no evidence MNTN can buy either [D; third-party, corrected].
- **Ease of use is now an API call:**
  - Amazon's ads interface for AI agents (MCP) has 700+ connections, Meta's ad connectors can both read and edit campaigns, and Vibe's MCP has 1,000+ brands [F; third-party].
  - MNTN's MCP is in beta with no disclosed usage, and "agentic" appears in none of its filings [F].
  - The CEO calls coordinating AI models "table stakes" [B].
- **Management answers with assertion, not data:** it calls tvScientific "not competitive in our sales cycles" and discloses no win rates, churn or take rate [B].

## 3. A hidden ~40% cut is the target
- MNTN "keeps a share of your media spend" and says "we don't disclose our margin" [H; Help Center].
- Rivals charge far less:
  - The Trade Desk (TTD) took ~20% for eight years [H; estimate from reported].
  - Google Buyer Direct caps fees at ~10%.
  - Amazon reportedly charges 0–1% [H; third-party].
- AI agents that compare cost per acquisition (CPA) across channels will expose a fee two to three times TTD's.
- Each point of take rate is worth ~2% of revenue. A 5-point cut takes the adj. EBITDA margin from ~28% to ~20% [H; estimate].
- No pressure is visible yet (gross margin is 80% [A; reported]), but because the cut is hidden, bulls cannot verify it either.

## 4. Attribution and alternative data
- **Attribution:** Verified Visits counts household-level visits after a TV view, within 7- or 14-day windows, with modeled outcomes blended in [M; reported]. The 10-K cites "minimal patent protection," and there is no MRC accreditation [N]. MNTN's own holdout tests and third-party integrations put its measurement on equal footing with everyone else's [M].
- **Alternative data all points the same way** [M; third-party, low confidence]:
  - Northbeam-tracked spend: Vibe +1,900% vs MNTN +29%.
  - Tranco web rank: mountain.com fell from 2,740 to 3,169, while vibe.co rose from 20,433 to 12,194.

## 5. Governance and share overhang
- **Control:** Class B shares hold ~66% of votes until 2032 [L; estimate].
- **Overhang:** pre-IPO holders own ~44% of shares, against ~0.7–1.0M of average daily volume. Qualcomm sold ~4.98M shares, and a director sold outside his 10b5-1 plan. No insider has bought [L; reported/estimate].
- **Disclosure quality:**
  - Net revenue retention (NRR) was replaced by an undefined "expansion rate" that counts only surviving customers [C].
  - A $10M-a-year deal paid in stock to an unnamed marketing contractor moves marketing cost into stock compensation (SBC), which adj. EBITDA adds back. Cash marketing spend fell at the same time [B/L; reported].
  - Headcount fell from 542 to 451 after layoffs, which went unmentioned on the calls [B].
  - The CEO claims "$100M of free cash flow"; 1H26 FCF was $27.0M [B; estimate].

## Why cheap is a trap
- The ~5.4x FY26 EBITDA multiple becomes 7.6x once SBC is counted as a cost [K; estimate].
- A reverse DCF that charges SBC implies +2.8–4.7% growth in perpetuity [K; estimate, corrected]. The price assumes survival, not decline.
- Ad-tech names that get cut out by the platforms see their multiples follow growth down:
  - TTD went from +18% growth to a Q3 guide of at least −12%. The stock is down 65% YTD at ~6x EBITDA [O].
  - Criteo's ex-TAC is down 13%, and it trades at ~1.5x [O].
- A Criteo multiple implies ~$5 [O; estimate].
- Our bear case (FY27 revenue of $358M vs $417M consensus) gives $4.96 by DCF and $6.78 from a 4x FY28 EBITDA exit [model v1; estimate].

## Catalysts
1. **Nov 4 Q3 results:** the guide implies Q4 revenue of ~$108M, up 24% [estimate], vs $108.4M [consensus]. The stock moves on the guide; it fell 23% on May 6 [K; third-party]. Watch for in-quarter customer growth below 24%.
2. **TTM convergence:** the +40% headline customer growth should fade toward the mid-20s through 2027 [C; estimate].
3. **FY27 guide (Feb 2027)** vs $417.3M [K; consensus]: it laps a 2026 boosted by one-off events. About 91–92% of revenue comes from small and mid-sized businesses on no committed contracts [J; reported].
4. **1H27:** Fox–Roku closes, and Walmart connects Vibe to its retail data [E/G].
5. **2027–28:** take-rate pressure arrives as agentic buying scales [F/H; estimate].

## What would make me cover
- In-quarter customer growth of 30%+ with flat revenue per customer, and an FY27 guide of at least $417M.
- A disclosed take rate, plus cohort and NRR data that hold up.
- Net adds above ~450 a quarter [N], or cash S&M falling as agents bring in customers more cheaply.
- A strategic bid, the main risk to the short: Walmart paid ~2x MNTN's EV for Vibe [G; third-party]. Short interest is ~9–10% of float [L], and net cash is $3.17 a share [K].
- The price reaching $5–7.
