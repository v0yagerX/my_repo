## Practitioner verdict: At Risk, score −1 (confidence about 55%)

**How likely each label is:** AI Victim 15%, At Risk 55%, Neutral 20%, Beneficiary 9%, AI Winner 1%. I put about 75% on At Risk or Neutral. The stock is at about 4.3–5.4x EV/EBITDA [estimate; K], which already prices in a decline like The Trade Desk's. So "At Risk" is close to what the market already assumes.

**How MNTN's buyer decides.** MNTN's customer comes from search and social. 95–97% have never bought TV [reported; C, 10-K], and more than 90% use no agency [reported; C]. They judge CTV the way they judge a Meta line item: by the return the platform reports. MNTN made TV workable for that buyer with three things:
- a self-serve interface;
- cheap creative;
- Verified Visits: it credits a site visit from anyone in the household within 7 days (retargeting) or 14 days (prospecting) of a completed TV view [reported; M, help center].

AI is wearing down all three:
- **Interface:** AI agents now plug straight into ad platforms through MCP connectors. Vibe has 1,000+ brands using its MCP [third-party; F, AdExchanger 2026-10-08]. MNTN's MCP is a beta with no usage disclosed [F].
- **Creative:** AI TV creative is now free or near-free on most platforms [I].
- **Attribution:** MNTN added 10% holdout tests and 8 third-party measurement integrations [reported; M]. As a practitioner I read that as admitting attribution is now a level playing field.

Two things remain: small budgets can reach premium private-marketplace inventory (">99% PMP" [management claim; B, D]), and the bidding is trained on conversions. Both are real. But they describe a tool that an agent calls and pays per outcome. They do not justify a destination charging an estimated 35–50% take rate [estimate, upper bound; A] (40–60% per [H]) when The Trade Desk takes about 21.6% [estimate; H, TTD 10-K].

**Why not AI Victim.** There is no damage in the numbers yet:
- Gross margin is 80.3% [reported; A].
- Concession allowances are flat at under 1% of revenue [reported; H].
- Receivables per dollar of revenue fell from 0.96 to 0.73, which does not point to take-rate compression [estimate; H].
- The Q3 guide implies +23–27% growth [guidance; G].

Falling CPMs give MNTN room to keep its spread and still improve customer returns, because it prices on a dynamic CPM. Streaming upfront CPMs are down about 5–7% [third-party; D], and Roku's price per impression fell 12% [reported; H]. Customers are fragmented, with the top 10 at about 20% of revenue [reported; C], so no holding company can force discounts the way The Trade Desk's large agency clients did.

**Why not Neutral.** The slowdown is in winning new customers, which is exactly where walled-garden defaults work:
- Customers active in the quarter grew +85% a year ago and +24% now [reported; C, 10-Q].
- Northbeam tracked spend shows Vibe +1,900% against MNTN +29% [third-party, published by Vibe; M].
- There is no evidence MNTN reaches YouTube or Amazon, which hold about 38% of streaming viewing time [estimate, corrected; D].
- Suppliers are merging and selling direct. Skydance–WBD closed Oct 6, 2026; Fox–Roku closes 1H27; Paramount Ads Manager advertises CTV from a $7 CPM [vendor claim; D].
- Amazon's DSP page now says "DVA+ brings the power of programmatic to all advertisers" [E].
- MNTN does not own its DSP layer; the 10-K says it uses "multiple vendors" [reported; F].
- Its agency push (agency-led accounts up about 4x, with cash-back rewards [H]) brings in buyers who expect to see fees.

### How MNTN compares with the four analogs

| Analog | What MNTN shares | Where it differs |
|---|---|---|
| **AppLovin** | Outcome-based machine-learning bidder; self-serve Ads Manager aimed at mid-market | AppLovin owns its supply and its signal, at an 84% EBITDA margin [reported; O]. MNTN rents its DSPs and its AI models. AppLovin's CEO calls CTV "step three" [reported; G, O]: a threat waiting in the wings, not a 2027 one. MNTN is not on the AppLovin path. |
| **The Trade Desk** | Independent buyer with no owned inventory, paid by take rate, exposed to Amazon's reported 0–1% fees [third-party; H] and to agents | Buyers are SMBs rather than holding companies, and MNTN still grows about 21% while The Trade Desk guides Q3 to at least −12% [guidance; O]. But MNTN's spread is about twice as large, so the same squeeze hurts more when it arrives. This is the closest template, running 2–3 years behind. |
| **Roku** | Self-serve TV for SMBs; Ads Manager advertisers more than doubled [reported; E] | Roku owns the home screen and OS data. After Fox closes it is both a supplier and a competitor. MNTN owns no supply. |
| **Magnite / PubMatic** | Same premium CTV supply; MNTN brings them new demand (PubMatic: +14% unique advertisers [vendor claim; D]) | They get paid whichever buyer wins, and they are building seller-side agents. This year the market rewarded them (PubMatic +100%, Magnite +48% YTD [reported; O]) and punished buy-side names. MNTN sits on the losing side of that split. |

### What would move the verdict one notch over 4–8 quarters

**Up to Neutral (0), if at least three of these happen:**
1. Customers active in the quarter grow 30% or more while spend per active customer is flat or up, and TTM net additions exceed 450 a quarter [N]. That would mean Express and QuickFrame are converting.
2. MNTN discloses spend or customers coming through MCP or agents, or Adobe or Google list MNTN as an activation partner [N].
3. Gross margin stays at 78% or above, receivables per dollar of revenue stay at 0.70 or above, and concessions stay flat as the agency mix grows [H].
4. Netflix or Prime Video access is confirmed, or NFL guaranteed access goes live. Fox–Roku and Skydance–WBD keep MNTN's private-marketplace terms after their deals close.
5. The first FY27 guide is +18–20% or better, against consensus of $417.3M [consensus; K].

**Down to AI Victim (−2), if at least two of these happen:**
1. Revenue growth stays below 15% for two quarters while CTV grows in the low teens, or quarterly-active customer growth falls below 15%.
2. Gross margin drops below 76%, MNTN discloses rebates or concessions, or it changes its pricing model to show fees.
3. A major supplier (Fox–Roku or Skydance–WBD) keeps SMB demand for its own self-serve tool, or MNTN loses an SSP or network deal.
4. Meta launches CTV audience extension through Magnite or FreeWheel, Amazon discloses SMB streaming-TV adoption, or Walmart keeps Vibe at $50 a day and its MCP keeps scaling.
5. S&M rises above 35% of revenue while incremental revenue per S&M dollar falls below 0.6x (it is 0.72x TTM [estimate; C]).
