## 0. Bottom line

1. **Revenue guidance is accurate but not conservative.** Across four guided quarters since the IPO, revenue landed between **0.0% and +1.9% above the guidance midpoint** [estimate]. Two of the four prints came in *below the high end*: Q3'25 and Q2'26.
   - The best "beat", Q1'26 at +1.9%, included **$1.7M of one-time revenue** from expired creative credits [reported, 10-Q]. Without it, Q1'26 revenue was ~$72.0M, **0.4% below the midpoint** [estimate].
   - Adj. EBITDA beats are larger but shrinking: +14%, +10%, +21% and then **+4.9%** vs midpoint in Q2'26, which was **below the high end** [estimate].
2. **FY2026 has been "raised" only once, by $2M,** and then reiterated.
   - Initial guide (Feb 10, 2026): $345–355M. Revised (May 5, 2026): $347–357M [guidance].
   - The raise roughly equals the Q1 beat, which itself included the $1.7M one-time item. The underlying rest-of-year outlook barely moved (+$0.6M) [estimate].
3. **Deceleration is real and steeper than the headline KPI shows.**
   - Revenue growth excluding Maximum Effort (ex-ME) fell from **36%** in FY25 [reported] to **21%** in Q2'26 [reported].
   - The headline TTM customer KPI still shows +40%. But **quarterly-active PTV customers grew only +24% y/y in Q2'26, vs +85% in Q2'25** (10-Q MD&A) [reported].
4. **Management's explanation.** Management frames the slowdown as:
   - a market moving from "early adopter" to "mainstream";
   - a go-to-market (GTM) rebuild that began in Q4'25 and will pay off in "6 to 12 months" and "meaningfully in 2027"; and
   - deliberate pacing of small-business onboarding.

   They rule out macro, politics and the World Cup as drivers.
5. **What management did not volunteer on the calls I reviewed:**
   - an April 28, 2026 restructuring of roughly 76 employees, which took headcount from 542 to 451 [reported, 10-Q];
   - a **y/y decline in marketing spend** in Q2'26 (–$1.3M) [reported, 10-Q]; and
   - a $10M stock-paid marketing contract with an unnamed contractor [reported, 10-Q].
6. **The 2H26 guide requires acceleration** to ~25% y/y from ~21% underlying in 1H26 [estimate].
7. **Q3 2026 is not yet reported.** Results are due **Wednesday, Nov 4, 2026, after market close**, with a webcast at 4:30 pm ET [reported, Business Wire Oct 14, 2026].

### 1d. Conclusion on reliability

- **Revenue guides are set close to the expected outcome**, with a cushion of roughly 0–1%, not a typical "beat-and-raise" setup.
  - Average revenue beat vs midpoint across the four guided quarters: **+1.0% reported, +0.6% excluding the one-time credit** [estimate: (0.0 + 1.3 + 1.9 + 0.7) / 4 = 0.98%].
  - No quarter beat the high end by more than 0.7%.
- **EBITDA beats come from cost control**, not revenue upside, and the cushion has narrowed: from +14–21% to +4.9%.
- **For the model:** treat the FY26 revenue midpoint of $352M as a central-to-optimistic case, not a floor. The 2H needs a ~3–4 percentage-point growth re-acceleration. MNTN's history is to land near the midpoint, so a reasonable FY26 band is **$345–354M [estimate]**.
- **Stock reactions** [third-party]:
  - After Q1'26: +7.4% in regular trading, plus +2.4% after hours to $10.83 (Investing.com).
  - After Q2'26: about –8% after hours, from $10.02 to $9.24, on GAAP EPS of $0.09 vs **$0.15 consensus** [consensus, per Investing.com, [Aug 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-mntn-q2-2026-profit-miss-sparks-afterhours-drop-93CH-4835959)].

### 2b. Management's explanations, by call

- **Market shift from early adopter to mainstream, which requires a rebuilt GTM** (Q1'26 Q&A; Q2'26 prepared remarks; Canaccord):
  - Q2'26, Douglas: "MNTN created this category, and now we're seeing Performance TV move from an early adopter market toward a mainstream part of the marketing mix. To capture that move, MNTN began expanding our sales organization in Q4 2025, strengthening our leadership in sales and increasing our investment in marketing."
  - Q2'26, Douglas: "The first half of this year has been focused on construction with additional leadership adding sales, marketing, and business development. We built vertical teams…"
  - Q2'26, Douglas: "we expect the benefits of our strategic investments to contribute to stronger growth in the second half of the year and meaningfully in 2027 as the core business accelerates, complemented by new revenue streams from Express and Premium." ([Fool transcript, call Aug 4, 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/mntn-mntn-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/))
  - Canaccord (Aug 11, 2026): "We have to do less evangelizing." Investments should "start to show up in the next 6 to 12 months." [third-party transcript]
- **Deliberate pacing of small-business adds and minimums.**
  - Q1'26, Douglas: "For small business, we want to ensure they come on, are successful, our acquisition costs are where we want them, and they have sustained growth. We constantly adjust how much small business we drive, product and reporting features, and marketing investment. We also control product minimums." ([Fool Q1'26, Q&A section](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/06/mntn-mntn-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/))
  - Pohlen's framing shifted between calls:
    - Q4'25 and Q1'26: customer count "is entirely within our control and is predominantly a function of how firmly we step on the accelerator to move down market."
    - Q2'26: "largely within our control and is primarily driven by how aggressively we choose to invest in sales and marketing… expected to fluctuate from quarter to quarter."
- **Lower spend per customer from mix shift toward small businesses** (10-Q language every quarter).
  - Small business was ~**6% of revenue in Q4'24** and ~**15% by Q3'25** (Douglas on the Q3'25 call, per [AdExchanger, Nov 5, 2025](https://www.adexchanger.com/tv/mntns-smb-ctv-ad-business-is-growing-like-gangbusters/)) [reported via third-party].
  - Small businesses spend "approximately about $15,000 a year", while mid-market spends "deep six figures" (Canaccord) [third-party transcript; a CEO estimate whose basis (gross spend or net revenue) is unclear].
- **Macro, tariffs and political are explicitly ruled out:**
  - Q1'26: "We are seeing essentially zero macro-driven pullback" (Fool) / "nearly zero impact from macro concerns" (Investing.com).
  - Q2'26, on political: "we traditionally have not participated in any significant way in political spending… it doesn't have much of a direct impact on MNTN."
  - On CPM volatility: "I think we're over 99% private marketplace deals," with pre-negotiated pricing.
- **World Cup is not in guidance.** Q1'26: "Being on all available content, including the World Cup, supports performance, but it is not a separate driver in our guidance."
- **Seasonality and comps.**
  - Q1 is "typically a seasonally slower quarter" (Pohlen, Q4'25 call).
  - Q4 was 30.0% of FY25 revenue and Q1 22.2% [reported, 10-Q].
  - Management did **not** cite the tough Q1'25 comp (+46%) explicitly in any transcript I could read. It is nonetheless arithmetically material.
- **Not explained on the calls I could read (MNTN-specific transcript text):**
  1. The **April 28, 2026 restructuring**, which reduced the workforce by about 76 employees and cost $1.5M. Headcount went from **542 (Mar 31, 2026) to 451 (Jun 30, 2026)**, versus 472 a year earlier [reported, 10-Qs].
  2. **Q2'26 marketing spend fell $1.3M y/y.** S&M growth (+17.5%) was driven by $3.4M of stock-based compensation from a "marketing services agreement… with a contractor", plus $1.2M of partnership commissions [reported, 10-Q].
  3. 1H26 S&M average headcount was **–2%** [reported].

  These sit awkwardly beside "aggressive" S&M investment and the 53 S&M hires announced on the Q4'25 call.

**My read [estimate].** The 36% to ~24% step-down is best explained by four things:
1. a deliberate slowdown in small-business onboarding after the 2025 surge (quarterly-active adds +85% to +24%);
2. a GTM rebuild in which the 53 Q4'25 hires were followed by a company-wide reduction in force (RIF) and new CRO / content leadership (Garland Hill, Peter Blacker; Q1'26 release);
3. the tough 1H25 comps; and
4. a one-time $1.7M flattering Q1'26.

Nothing in the disclosures attributes the slowdown to AI disintermediation or to walled-garden share loss. But MNTN provides **no win/loss, churn or take-rate data** that would let an outsider rule that out.

## 4. Customers and products

- **Customer counts:** see the table in 2a.
- **Expansion rate:** "well north of 115%" (spend of current customers vs the same customers a year ago), repeated Q3'25 through Q2'26 [reported, qualitative bound]. It is never quantified, and no logo retention is disclosed (data gap).
- **Concentration:** top-10 customers were ~17% of FY25 revenue and **~20% of 1H26** (18% in FY24). Almost no customers have committed contracts [reported, 10-K / 10-Q].
- **Unit economics:** "LTV CAC ratios that are handily in the double digits on the majority of our mid-market customers" (Q4'25) [reported, qualitative].
- **Lead mix:** inbound leads were ~2% of leads three years ago and **>75%** now (Q3'25 call) [reported via third-party].
- **Agencies:**
  - Agency-led accounts "quadrupled in 2025" (Q3'25 release).
  - ">90% of performance TV advertisers do not use an agency"; the agencies MNTN works with are independent performance shops (Q1'26).
- **Minimum spend:**
  - Q1'26: "We also control product minimums."
  - Q2'25 (garbled): campaign minimums were once $25,000 per month (current figure unintelligible).
