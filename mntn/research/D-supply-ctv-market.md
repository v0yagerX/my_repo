## 1. Executive summary

1. **How MNTN buys.** MNTN buys "through both negotiated arrangements directly with premium TV networks and Supply Side Platforms" [reported, [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1891027/000189102726000019/mntn-20251231.htm)]. The CEO says MNTN is "over 99% private marketplace deals" and "we don't buy open market inventory" [reported, management claim; [Q2'26 call, 2026-08-04](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/mntn-mntn-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [AdExchanger, 2026-08-07](https://www.adexchanger.com/ctv-roundup/why-mntns-ceo-isnt-sweating-media-consolidation/)]. The SSPs are the pipes: **Magnite** (Jan 2026; adds Disney live inventory, pause ads and home-screen units) and **PubMatic** (Oct 2025; Paramount, NBCU, Sling, Philo, LG Channels). The marketing claim is "150+ direct deals" with networks [reported, claim; [mountain.com](https://mountain.com/performance-tv/inventory/), accessed 2026-10-15].
2. **What MNTN can reach.** Named supply covers NBCU (Peacock, Bravo, Telemundo World Cup), Paramount (CBS, Paramount+), WBD (HBO Max), Fox, Disney/ESPN (via Magnite), AMC, The CW, sports leagues, and a Roku logo. **What it does not demonstrably reach** is three platforms: **YouTube** (14.2% of all US TV viewing, July 2026), **Netflix** (7.8%) and **Amazon/Prime Video** (4.3%). Together they hold about **54% of US streaming viewing time** [estimate: (14.2+7.8+4.3)/49.0 = 53.7%; Nielsen data via [TheWrap, 2026-09-10](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/streaming/youtube-viewership-july-2026/)]. Netflix access is **unverified and conflicting** (see §2.3).
3. **Inventory costs.** CTV CPMs are deflating, but this does **not** hit MNTN's reported gross margin directly. Revenue is recognized **net** of media cost, and cost of revenue is hosting, data, platform fees, personnel and amortization [reported, 10-K]. Q2'26 gross margin was **80%** [reported], with cost of revenue up only 2.2% y/y while revenue grew 21% [reported, [10-Q Q2'26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1891027/000189102726000073/mntn-20260630.htm)]. Falling CPMs (2026-27 upfront streaming CPM **$25.90 vs $27.75** [third-party, Media Dynamics via [MediaPost, 2026-08-11](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417166/prime-time-upfront-ad-sales-rise-9-to-337b-cpm.html)]) mean **about 7% more impressions per advertiser dollar** [estimate: 27.75/25.90 − 1]. That supports SMB ROAS and retention.
4. **Supply is consolidating, and walled gardens are gaining.** Paramount Skydance **closed its WBD acquisition on Oct 6, 2026** [reported, [WBD 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1437107/000119312526415055/d200872d8k.htm)]. **Fox agreed to buy Roku** for about $22B EV, with closing expected 1H27 [reported, [Fox/Roku release, 2026-06-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1428439/000114036126025115/ef20076172_ex99-1.htm)]. Amazon DSP has secured Roku (about 80M US households), Netflix and Disney DRAX integrations [third-party, [Marketing Dive, 2025-09-09](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/netflix-ads-come-to-amazon-dsp-as-streaming-race-evolves/759706/)]. Amazon ads grew **+26%** in Q2'26 while The Trade Desk grew **+3%** [reported]. Paramount, one of MNTN's own suppliers, now runs a **self-serve SMB "Ads Manager" that starts at a $7 CPM** [third-party/vendor claim, [Paramount Ads Manager](https://adsmanager.paramount.com/insights/ctv-advertising-cost)].
5. **Market growth.** US CTV ad spend is about **$33B (2025) → about $38B (2026, +14.5%) → about $47B (2028) → $51–53B (2029)**, all eMarketer [third-party]. My extrapolation puts 2030 at **$56–59B** [estimate]. Linear TV is shrinking (national TV **−2%**, local **−3%** in 2026 per Madison and Wall; IAB linear **−1.5%**). Streaming is **49.0%** of TV time (Nielsen, July 2026). MNTN's **net** revenue is about **0.9%** of US CTV spend [estimate].
6. **AI read for this topic: neutral to mildly positive (about +0.5 on a −5/+5 scale).** CTV demand is growing faster than ads overall, and CPM deflation helps performance buyers. AI cannot create TV inventory, so the 150+ direct deals are a non-AI asset. The ceiling comes from the three biggest streaming pools (YouTube, Netflix, Amazon), which sit with walled gardens that are building AI-assisted self-serve for SMBs. Publishers' own self-serve tools are the disintermediation vector.

**Read.** The CEO's "direct deal + programmatic pipe" description, combined with the 10-K's reliance on "multiple vendors to serve as its demand-side platform," suggests a layered chain. MNTN negotiates deal IDs and pricing with networks. Impressions arrive through SSPs (Magnite, PubMatic). At least part of the bidding runs on third-party DSP infrastructure, which shows up as volume-linked "platform fees" in cost of revenue [estimate/inference]. Two data points suggest MNTN is in-sourcing that layer [estimate/inference]:
- FY2025 hosting rose because of "new products, including bidding-related software" [reported, 10-K].
- Q2'26 platform fees **fell $0.6M y/y** while revenue rose 21% [reported, 10-Q Q2'26].

### 3.2 What this means for MNTN

1. **Reported gross margin is mostly insulated.** MNTN reports revenue "net of payments to suppliers" because it "does not control the advertising inventory and … does not set the price" [reported, 10-K]. Cost of revenue "consists primarily of hosting and data costs, third-party service fees, and personnel costs" [reported]. The record on recent margin moves:
   - FY2025 cost of revenue rose $2.1M to $66.2M (hosting +$2.3M, platform fees +$2.9M, data fees +$2.9M, creative −$7.8M) on revenue of **$290.1M (+28.6%)**, for a gross margin of about **77%** [reported; GM = 1 − 66.2/290.1].
   - Q2'26 cost of revenue was **$16.2M (+2.2%)**, helped by lower platform fees (−$0.6M) and a **38% cut in cost-of-revenue headcount**. Gross margin was **80%**, up 350bp [reported, 10-Q / 8-K].
   - The long-term gross-margin target is **75–80%** [guidance, Q4'25 call].
   - CPM deflation is therefore **not** the gross-margin driver. Hosting vendor changes, restructuring and Maximum Effort's exit are.
2. **Advertisers get more reach per dollar** [estimate]:
   - At the Media Dynamics streaming average, $1,000 buys 1,000/25.90 × 1,000 ≈ **38,600** impressions vs ≈ **36,000** a year earlier (+7.1%).
   - On Netflix in 2024, the gain was +35.7% (42.14/31.05 − 1).
   - For performance buyers whose ROAS scales with impressions per dollar, deflation is a tailwind to results, retention and expansion. MNTN reports net revenue retention "well north of 115%" [reported, Q2'26 call].
3. **Take rate is the unknown.** MNTN says that as spend grows it can "negotiate preferential ad inventory pricing on [customers'] behalf," and that networks "often reduce inventory prices to increase demand" [reported, 10-K]. Whether those discounts go to advertisers (more impressions) or to MNTN (a wider spread) is **not disclosed**. Gross billings and take rate are not reported [data gap]. A sibling dossier triangulates about **40–46%** from receivables and payables (A-filings-financials, [estimate], low confidence).
4. **The $7-CPM risk.** If publisher self-serve (Paramount Ads Manager, Roku Ads Manager) and Amazon reset SMB price expectations far below MNTN's effective all-in CPM, MNTN must defend its fee with measurably better ROAS. Otherwise its take rate compresses. This is the main inventory-cost risk to the **revenue** line, as opposed to gross margin [qualitative/estimate].
5. **Premium push vs. cost.** The "guaranteed access" beta (live sports, specific shows) is the opposite of buying remnant. It likely carries **higher CPMs**. Management argues performance buyers "are actually willing and interested to pay a higher price for an ad slot" if it converts [reported, Q4'25 call]. The help center says MNTN does not pre-buy inventory, so committed-volume risk looks low today. That should be re-checked if guaranteed access scales [data gap].

## 4. Supply concentration and loss risk

### 4.1 MNTN-specific concentration

- **No supplier-level concentration figures are disclosed.** There are no top-SSP or top-network percentages [reported absence, 10-K / 10-Q]. Customer concentration is modest: the top 10 customers were about **17%** of 2025 revenue [reported, 10-K].
- The 10-K warns: "The number of large customers and sellers of advertising inventory in the market is finite, and it could be difficult for us to replace the losses from any … sellers of advertising inventory whose relationships with us diminish or terminate." It adds that sellers "may allocate their advertising inventory … to our competitors who provide advertising demand and supply to them on more favorable terms" [reported, risk factors].
- Intermediary risk: MNTN is "unable to prevent technological intermediaries from aggregating bid requests from customers and directing it to their own buying platforms" [reported, 10-K]. This is notable because SSPs and DSP vendors sit in MNTN's path.
- A **$3.5M vendor credit** from a single, unnamed vendor (2025 purchases, applied in 2026) shows at least one supplier relationship is large enough to generate meaningful rebates [reported, 10-K Note; 10-Q Q2'26].

### 4.4 Diversification assessment

- **Strengths:**
  - 150+ direct network deals (claim).
  - At least two independent SSPs (Magnite, PubMatic).
  - Over 99% PMP, which insulates pricing from open-market spikes.
  - Broad sports coverage via media companies that hold the rights.
  - Suppliers say incremental SMB demand lifts their revenue (PubMatic: +10% for the publishers in its test).
- **Weaknesses:**
  - No named FreeWheel or Index Exchange relationship.
  - No Netflix, YouTube or Prime Video confirmation.
  - Reliance on third-party DSP vendors.
  - Suppliers are consolidating: Paramount+WBD done, Fox+Roku pending. Fewer, bigger sellers have more leverage and their own SMB self-serve tools.
- **Net:** moderate supply risk. **Losing any single network is survivable** (no disclosed concentration). The structural risk is **gradual**: the largest pools stay closed, and the open pools get discounted directly to SMBs by their owners.

## 6. Thesis implications: AI victim or AI winner (this topic)

**Verdict: neutral to mildly AI-winner-leaning, about +0.5 on a −5 to +5 scale. Medium confidence.**

**Positives:**
- **Inventory is a physical, rights-based asset AI cannot replicate.** Premium episodic TV and live sports belong to a handful of media companies. MNTN's 150+ network deals, PMP pricing and sports access (World Cup, March Madness, NBA/MLB/NHL) are relationship assets.
- **The CTV pie grows about 11–16% a year** while linear shrinks, and streaming is now about half of TV time.
- **CPM deflation (about −5% to −7% in the 2026-27 upfront) makes each SMB dollar go further**, which supports ROAS. It does not hit MNTN's net-revenue gross margin, which reached 80% in Q2'26.
- **AI creative tools widen the funnel** of SMBs able to make TV ads. That is incremental demand for MNTN's suppliers (PubMatic: +14% unique advertisers, +10% revenue for publishers in its test).

**Negatives:**
- **The three largest streaming pools (YouTube, Netflix, Prime Video; about 54% of streaming time) are not demonstrably available to MNTN.** Their owners are building AI-driven buying (Amazon Ads Agent and MCP, Netflix "testing AI agents to … purchase ads," YouTube via Google Ads).
- **Suppliers are consolidating** (Paramount+WBD done; Fox+Roku pending) and **launching their own SMB self-serve**: Paramount Ads Manager from a $7 CPM, Roku Ads Manager, and Netflix programmatic for "smaller buyers." AI-built interfaces lower the cost of these direct channels, which is the disintermediation risk the 10-K itself flags.
- **Amazon is taking buy-side share** (+26% vs TTD +3%), with privileged pipes into Roku, Netflix and Disney.

**For the 2026–2030 model:**
- Market: US CTV **$37–38B (2026) → $56–59B (2030)** [estimate].
- MNTN net-revenue share: **0.9% (2026)**, with **1.0% / 1.5% / 2.0%** as bear / base / bull 2030 markers. That gives 2030 revenue of about **$0.56B / $0.84–0.89B / $1.12–1.19B** [estimate].
- Gross margin: hold at **75–80%** [guidance], independent of CPMs.
- Explicit risks to haircut: supplier loss or tightening after mergers, and take-rate compression from publisher self-serve.
