What the annual numbers show:

- **Gross margin** rose from 54% to 77% between FY2022 and FY2025 [reported]. Hosting costs fell $11.5M in 2023 after one-time 2022 development work [reported, DRS/A]. The low-margin Maximum Effort agency was divested; it ran a 33% gross margin in FY2024 [reported]. Vendor and data renegotiations also helped.
- **Most of the operating-leverage story comes from S&M and G&A.** S&M fell from 53.5% of revenue in FY2022 to 31.2% in FY2025, and G&A fell from 49.3% to 19.9% [estimate from reported lines]. T&D is now the fastest-growing cost line: +50.8% in FY2025 and +52% y/y in 1H26 [reported].
- **SBC was dominated by G&A awards granted in 2021**, including CEO performance options and promissory-note awards. Those awards finished vesting in 2025, which is why SBC dropped sharply in 2026 (see §2).
- **The FY2025 GAAP loss is driven by IPO-related non-cash items.** The company calls out a "one-time charge of $23.0 million related to the Company's IPO" [reported, Q2'25/Q4'25 releases]. My reconciliation: $26.4M loss on extinguishment, plus $4.4M fair-value loss on the converts, minus a $7.8M gain on the Series D warrants = $23.0M [estimate from reported 10-Q components].

## 3. Revenue recognition, cost of revenue and how MNTN charges

### 3a. Net (agent) presentation: the key accounting fact

The 10-K revenue policy reads [reported, 10-K FY2025 Note 2, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1891027/000189102726000019/mntn-20251231.htm]:

> "The Company generates revenue by charging its customers a variable usage-based fee based on the level of ad spend and through charging fees for various ad production activities."

> "The Company has determined that it does not act as the principal in the purchase and sale of digital advertising inventory because it does not control the advertising inventory and it does not set the price which is the result of an auction within the marketplace. Based on these and other factors, the Company reports revenue from the sale of advertising inventory on its platform on a net basis. The Company has also determined that it does not act as the principal in its production activities because the Company's role as a facilitator does not give the Company complete control over the specified good, is not primarily responsible for the performance of third-party services, cannot redirect those services to fulfill other contracts, does not carry inventory risk, and does not set the price of third party services used in the production activities. Therefore, the Company also reports revenue from its production activities on a net basis."

> "The Company bills its customers on a gross basis, inclusive of the cost of procuring the advertising inventory. The Company reports revenue on a net basis which represents gross billings net of amounts it pays suppliers for the cost of advertising inventory. ... Accordingly, both accounts receivable and accounts payable appear large in relation to revenue reported on a net basis."

MD&A gives the same definition: revenue is "usage-based fees from customers based on their level of ad spend on our platform, net of amounts paid to suppliers for the cost of advertising inventory" [reported]. MNTN is therefore an **agent: media cost is NOT in revenue or cost of revenue**. The same policy wording appears in the 2024 DRS/A and the 424B4.

### 3b. What sits in cost of revenue

From 10-K Note 2 and the MD&A [reported]: "hosting and data costs, third-party service fees, and personnel costs" for platform-support staff and video-production staff. It also includes SBC ($0.6M in FY2025) and **amortization of capitalized internal-use software**: $7.1M in FY2025 and $5.7M in FY2024, on 3-year straight-line lives. Write-offs of obsolete software also run through COGS. The scheduled amortization of the existing balance is $7.5M in 2026, $6.2M in 2027, $3.6M in 2028 and $0.5M in 2029 [reported].

The FY2025 cost bridge [reported]: hosting +$2.3M ("bidding-related software"), **platform fees +$2.9M "driven by the increase in revenue volume"**, data +$2.9M, IUS amortization +$1.5M, and ME creative costs −$7.8M.

The concentration note says MNTN "relies on multiple vendors to serve as its demand-side platform from which customers can purchase advertising inventory, which account for a significant portion of costs" [reported]. The volume-linked "platform fees" are therefore most likely fees to these DSP and tech intermediaries [estimate/inference].

Items NOT in COGS:
- Merchant (credit-card) fees sit in **G&A**; they rose $2.3M in FY2025 "driven by increased revenue volume" [reported].
- Bad-debt expense is in G&A.
- Customer credits reduce revenue.
- Ad inventory itself is netted out of revenue.

### 3c. What MNTN charges for

- **PTV: a variable, usage-based fee tied to customer ad spend.** The customer sees one media price that includes MNTN's fee. The CEO on the Q2'26 call: "We want our customers to have an experience where there is one cost to using MNTN, which is the cost of media" (Investing.com transcript, Aug 2026; not checked against the recording).
  - Over 99% of inventory is bought through **private-marketplace deals** (Q2'26 call, via The Motley Fool).
  - The platform now has three tiers: Express, Pro and Premium. MNTN Express launched Apr 1, 2026 for small businesses and had over 7,000 sign-ups in 120 days (Q2'26 call).
  - **No take rate, gross billings or price list is disclosed** [reported absence].
  - Customers generally have **no committed contracts** ("almost all of our customers are not subject to committed contracts") [reported, 10-K risk factors].
- **Creative and production fees.** These come from QuickFrame (a marketplace of "thousands of independent creators") and, until Apr 1, 2025, from Maximum Effort. They are recognized on a net basis for production [reported].
  - Creative can be bundled for annual contracts with minimum spend, earned through spend thresholds, or bought outright [reported, 10-K risk factor].
  - Unused creative credits sit in deferred revenue: $5.5M at YE2025 against $9.0M at YE2024 [reported]. Their expiry produced the $1.7M Q1'26 breakage.
- **QuickFrame AI** (generative-video tool) is **currently free**. The CEO: "at this moment, we're making it available at no charge ... Stay tuned on that" and "It's essentially gross margin neutral" (Q2'26 call via Investing.com). It had about 37,000 sign-ups in Q2'26 and over 73,000 YTD (The Motley Fool summary).
- **QuickFrame and creative revenue size.** It is not broken out as a line. By derivation it is about $2.8M in FY2025 and about $0.7M a quarter in 2025 [estimate: Q2'25 total $68.46M less PTV $67.8M; Q1'25 total $64.5M less ME $5.4M less PTV $58.3M = $0.8M]. That makes it **≈1% of revenue**, so it functions as a customer-acquisition tool rather than a profit center. Q2'26 PTV revenue rose $14.2M while total revenue rose $14.1M [reported], so non-PTV revenue edged down again.

### 3d. Why an ~80% gross margin fits the policy, and what it means on a gross basis

Revenue is MNTN's net fee after paying for media. COGS is only technology delivery (hosting, data, DSP/platform fees, ad-ops staff, software amortization). The 80% gross margin is therefore a margin on the *take*, not on media spend, and it is not comparable with gross-recognizing ad sellers.

The take rate is not disclosed. Approximate triangulation [estimate]:
- AR is recorded at gross billings: $61.8M at YE2025 [reported], with typical "net 30" terms [reported]. If DSO is about 30 days, Q4'25 gross billings ≈ 61.8 × 92/30 ≈ $190M, implying a net take of about 87.1/190 ≈ **46%**.
- AP and accrued expenses were $59.5M [reported], which includes non-media accruals. If $45–55M is media payables at 30–60 days DPO, Q4 media cost is about $70–170M, implying a take of about **35–55%**.
- Third-party estimates sit in the same range. Quo Vadis (Tom Triscari, May 27, 2025) assumed 70 payable days and derived FY2024 gross spend of about $557M, a **~40% take rate** [third-party, https://www.quovadisnews.com/p/119-mntn-ipo-valuation-update].

My working range is **35–50%** [estimate], against roughly 20% for The Trade Desk-style DSPs (from memory; TTD's own filings would need checking). On a gross-billings basis, gross margin would be about 0.80 × 35–50% ≈ **28–40%** [estimate].

**Implication:** a high take rate is a large pool of economics. AI-driven price transparency, self-serve tools from walled gardens, or agentic buying could compress it (§8).

### 3e. Segment, geography, concentration and RPO [reported]

- **One operating and reportable segment, "entirely within the United States."** The CODM (the CEO) reviews consolidated net income or loss (10-K Note 16; Q2'26 10-Q Note 13).
- **No customer above 10% of revenue** in FY2022–FY2025; "no major concentrations of accounts receivable or revenue." The **top ten customers were ~17% of FY2025 revenue (~18% in FY2024)** (10-K risk factor). In FY2024, 421 customers generated over $100k of net revenue, against 297 in FY2023 [reported, 424B4].
- **Customer mix.** SMBs were 92% of FY2024 PTV revenue, of which 86% came from mid-sized businesses. In Q1'25, small businesses were 12% and mid-sized businesses 79% [reported, 424B4]. About 95–96% of customers had never advertised on TV before [reported].
- **Remaining performance obligations:** none disclosed. The closest proxy is deferred revenue of $5.5M at YE2025, expected to be recognized within 12 months [reported].
- **Seasonality.** Q4 was 30.0% of FY2025 revenue and Q1 was 22.2% [reported].

## 8. Risk factors on AI, walled gardens, supply and competition (10-K FY2025 and Q2'26 10-Q)

These are summarized and closely paraphrased, with short quotes. All are [reported].

1. **Disintermediation by sellers and buyers (the walled-garden risk).**
   - "Some existing and potential customers have their own relationships with sellers or are seeking to establish such relationships, and many sellers are investing in capabilities that enable them to connect more effectively directly with customers. Our business suffers to the extent that customers and sellers purchase and sell advertising inventory directly from one another or through intermediaries other than us."
   - Customers "may bypass us and transact directly with sellers of advertising inventory or through other intermediaries that compete with us."
2. **Competitors with scale and data.**
   - Rivals "may also be able to sell products or services competitive to ours with more attractive value propositions given proprietary ownership of data, technical superiority, or economies of scale."
   - "Our competitors or potential competitors may adopt certain aspects of our business model."
   - Market saturation "may change the competitive landscape in favor of larger competitors."
   - The filings name no competitors.
3. **Reliance on tech intermediaries (DSP vendors) and supply.**
   - MNTN "relies on multiple vendors to serve as its demand-side platform ... which account for a significant portion of costs," and a switch "may" cause short-term disruption.
   - "We are unable to prevent technological intermediaries from aggregating bid requests from customers and directing it to their own buying platforms or even reselling such bid data."
   - Relationships with "large sources of supply that have their own monetization capabilities ... may be less profitable because we may be required to compensate these partners or share the fees available for intermediaries."
   - "We generally do not have fixed commitments with customers or sellers of advertising inventory."
4. **AI-specific risks.** The 2026 10-Qs expand these and define "AI Technologies."
   - **Deployment risk:** MNTN Matched and generative creative may fail if models are flawed or trained on poor or unlicensed data.
   - **Dependence on licensed third-party models and hosted services:** "if the providers of such models unfavorably change the terms ... our solutions may become less appealing."
   - **Licensed training data** may not be available on reasonable terms.
   - **Generative output** may infringe third-party IP or be non-copyrightable.
   - **Regulation** is evolving.
   - **New in the 2026 10-Qs:** "Our development of proprietary AI Technologies is costly and complex." It cites model drift, compute and data dependence, and "significant competition from other companies in our industry in relation to the development and deployment of AI Technologies. Those other companies may develop AI Technologies that are similar or superior to ours and/or are more cost-effective and/or quicker to develop."
5. **Creative strategy risk.** The risk factor says MNTN "may not be able to generate enough revenue to offset our cost of the creative solutions." Creative is bundled with minimum-spend contracts or spend thresholds.
6. **Identity and signal loss.**
   - Apple and Google identifier restrictions (Privacy Sandbox, Android ad ID deprecation) could degrade MNTN's targeting and measurement.
   - Pixel restrictions and **VPPA / CIPA wiretap litigation** against ad-tech intermediaries "may reduce demand for our technology."
7. **Concentration and no contracts.** The top ten customers are about 17% of revenue and "almost all of our customers are not subject to committed contracts."
8. **Governance.** The dual-class structure and CEO dependence. The corporate-opportunity waiver for non-employee directors and stockholders matters because the VCs hold Class B shares.

## 9. Takeaways for the AI victim vs AI winner question (filings lens only)

- **Supports "winner":**
  - The P&L is already high-margin and self-funding: 80% gross margin, 26% LTM adj. EBITDA margin, about 19% LTM FCF margin, $237M net cash.
  - AI is being used both to lower the *cost to serve*, with COR headcount down 38% y/y, and to lower the *creative barrier* through a free QuickFrame AI.
  - Underlying SBC has fallen to about 5–7% of revenue.
  - Cash taxes stay low through about 2027.
- **Supports "victim / at-risk":**
  - Revenue is a **net fee on media**, plausibly 35–50% of gross spend [estimate]. This is exactly the economics that walled gardens' self-serve tools, AI-agent buying and price transparency could compress. The filings explicitly flag sellers going direct and intermediaries diverting bid data.
  - Growth is **decelerating**: ex-ME growth fell from 46% in Q1'25 to 21% in Q2'26, and Q1'26 was flattered by $1.7M of breakage.
  - **Quarterly active customer growth slowed from about 85% to 24%**, and spend per customer falls every period.
  - Management prioritizes "investing behind growth rather than optimizing near-term adjusted EBITDA margins."
  - Creative monetization is near zero.
- **Net read:** the financial statements show a profitable, cash-generative niche intermediary whose *unit economics* (falling spend per customer, slowing cohort growth) are the swing factor. The AI outcome will show up in **take rate and spend per customer**, neither of which is disclosed. This topic is neutral to modestly negative on AI exposure: the accounting is clean and the cash quality is good, but disintermediation risk sits directly in the revenue line.
